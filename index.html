Thread 1

Course development and pedagogy

Whether to teach AI is settled. The open questions are where it lives in the curriculum, who teaches it, and how students practice using it critically without outsourcing their thinking.

Questions to consider

  1. Standalone AI/data science course, threaded across existing courses, or both? What did your program choose, and what would you change?
  2. What is the minimum AI literacy every BSW/MSW graduate needs, and what belongs only in an elective or doctoral course?
  3. Which assignment has worked best for teaching students to critique AI output rather than just use it?
  4. Who teaches this at your school, and what support (release time, co-teaching with CS or data science, workshops) made it possible?

Thread 2

Competency alignment and evaluation with the 2029 EPAS

CSWE's 2025 Technology Task Force recommended making technology prominent in the 2029 EPAS. Draft 1 does both: it threads AI through existing competencies and proposes a new one. The draft is a moving target, so this is a good moment to map courses and plan assessment.

Where AI shows up in Draft 1

CompetencyAI and technology emphasis
C1 EthicsUse emerging technologies and AI ethically, and understand how they shape the practice environment.
C4 ResearchEthical use of technology in the research process.
C5 PolicyAnalyze how AI and digital-system policies affect privacy, self-determination, and access to services.
C7 AssessmentCritically evaluate how digital technology affects assessment.
C8 InterventionUse technology ethically and appropriately in interventions.
C9 EvaluationRecognize the opportunities and limits of data, AI, and digital systems.
C10 TechnologyNew: evaluate, select, and integrate technology, including AI, and examine its effect on access to resources.

Paraphrased from the CSWE 2029 EPAS Draft 1 page (Oct 2026). Check the draft for exact wording before using it in course documents.

Questions to consider

  1. A dedicated Competency 10 or AI woven through C1 to C9: which serves students better, and which is easier to assess?
  2. What would a valid, observable behavior for "selects and integrates AI ethically" look like in field? Who rates it?
  3. How are you assessing AI-related learning now: rubrics, simulations, portfolios, field evaluations?
  4. What feedback will you or your program submit on the draft before November 20?

Thread 3

Classroom and PhD program AI policies informed by ETHICS

The ETHICS framework (Lee et al., 2025, JSSWR) sets six principles for AI use in social work research. The same principles give a structure for course syllabi and doctoral program guidance, where students are both learners and emerging researchers.

PrincipleIn a course syllabusIn a PhD program
EEquityEnsure access to approved tools so policy does not reward students who can pay for premium AI.Weigh who benefits from AI-enabled studies; avoid biased samples and data.
TTransparencyRequire a short AI-use statement on assignments: tool, purpose, and what the student changed.Disclose AI use in proposals, IRB materials, and dissertations, with its limits.
HHuman-centerednessKeep client and field information out of AI tools; protect the relational skills AI cannot practice for you.Explain AI's role in consent and keep human oversight of AI-assisted coding and analysis.
IIntegrityThe student is accountable for every claim and citation; verify all AI output.AI supports but does not replace the scholar; build ongoing AI literacy into training.
CCollaborationCo-create the policy with students in week one and revisit it mid-term.Set data governance and authorship expectations with mentors and community partners.
SSafetyName clear stop points, such as no AI with case notes or identifiable data.Set stop/go criteria for AI use and pause when privacy or stigma risks appear.

The columns apply ETHICS to teaching and doctoral training as a starting point for discussion. The framework itself was written for research.

Starter syllabus language

AI use in this course follows the ETHICS principles (Equity, Transparency, Human-centeredness, Integrity, Collaboration, Safety).

You may use [approved tools] for [brainstorming, feedback on drafts, coding help]. AI use is not permitted for [reflection papers, exams, any task involving client or field information].

When you use AI, add a two-sentence note to your submission: which tool, for what, and what you changed. You are responsible for the accuracy of everything you submit, including citations.

Never enter identifiable client, agency, or field placement information into an AI tool. If you are unsure whether a use is appropriate, ask first. We will revisit this policy together at mid-semester.

Questions to consider

  1. Is your current syllabus policy about permission (allowed or banned) or about practice (how to use AI well)? Which ETHICS principle is missing?
  2. Should a PhD program policy differ from a course policy? Think about comprehensive exams, dissertations, and AI as a research method versus a writing aid.
  3. How do you handle field: agency rules, client data, and AI documentation tools students already see at placement?
  4. Who owns the policy at your school: individual faculty, the program, or the university? What is working?
